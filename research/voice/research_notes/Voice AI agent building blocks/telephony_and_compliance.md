## Q1. Telephony stack: SIP trunking vs programmable-voice media streams, audio formats, latency, DTMF, transfer, AMD, recording, numbers, caller-ID reputation, costs

### Takeaway
There are two ways onto the PSTN. (a) A programmable-voice CPaaS (Twilio, Telnyx, Plivo, Vonage, SignalWire) forks the call audio over a WebSocket. For Twilio that is base64 8 kHz mono μ-law, bidirectional only with `<Connect><Stream>`. Some CPaaS products also do STT/TTS for you and exchange text (Twilio ConversationRelay at $0.07/min, Telnyx Conversation Relay at $0.05/min). (b) Raw SIP trunking into your own media server or agent framework. At list price the telephony leg is cheap, roughly $0.01–0.02/min all-in on Twilio and about half that on Telnyx. STT, TTS and the LLM dominate per-minute cost. The engineering risks sit elsewhere: the 8 kHz codec, the hop or transcoding latency budget, AMD accuracy and timing, and caller-ID reputation (STIR/SHAKEN attestation plus analytics-vendor spam labels).

### Cited Findings

**Media-stream audio format and protocol (Twilio Media Streams, primary vendor docs)**
- Twilio Media Streams sends inbound audio as `audio/x-mulaw`, 8000 Hz, 1 channel, base64 in `media.payload`. Audio sent back must also be mulaw/8000 base64 and "should not contain audio file type header bytes" — [Twilio Media Streams WebSocket messages](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/media-streams/websocket-messages)
- Twilio→app messages are `connected`, `start` (metadata incl. `tracks`), `media` (tagged `inbound`/`outbound`), `dtmf`, `stop` and `mark`. App→Twilio messages are `media` (play audio), `mark` (notify when the preceding audio finishes) and `clear` (flush buffered audio, which is the barge-in primitive) — [Twilio Media Streams WebSocket messages](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/media-streams/websocket-messages)
- Only streams started with `<Connect><Stream>` are bidirectional. `mark` and `dtmf` messages exist only on bidirectional streams. DTMF arrives as a `dtmf` message with `dtmf.digit`, always on the inbound track. A bidirectional stream ends when the call ends or the server closes the socket — [Twilio Media Streams WebSocket messages](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/media-streams/websocket-messages)
- Async AMD uses one of **four** per-call forked audio streams, a budget shared with Media Streams, SIPREC and Real-Time Transcription — [Twilio AMD docs](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/answering-machine-detection)

**Other CPaaS audio formats**
- Plivo `<Stream>` supports μ-law 8 kHz, linear PCM 8 kHz and linear PCM 16 kHz, and recommends μ-law for telephony. `bidirectional="true"` lets the server send audio back with a `playAudio` event that carries content type, sample rate and base64 payload. Plivo's pages disagree on which format is the default — [Plivo audio streaming guide](https://plivo.com/docs/voice-agents/audio-streaming/concepts/audio-streaming-guide); [Plivo XML audio streaming](https://plivo.com/docs/voice/xml/audio-streaming)
- Vonage Voice API WebSockets (NCCO `connect` action) offer 16-bit linear PCM at 8, 16 or 24 kHz, and Vonage recommends 16 kHz for speech recognition (search summary of the docs. The bidirectional details were not verified) — [Vonage WebSockets guide](https://developer.vonage.com/en/voice/voice-api/guides/websockets)

**Speech-to-text-and-back as a service (text-over-WebSocket)**
- Twilio ConversationRelay does STT and TTS for you, and your app exchanges text. TwiML is `<Connect action=…><ConversationRelay url=… welcomeGreeting=…/>`. Listed providers: Deepgram (STT, Nova-2 base), Google (STT+TTS), Amazon Polly (TTS), ElevenLabs (TTS Flash 2/2.5) — [Twilio ConversationRelay](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/conversationrelay)
- ConversationRelay messages. Twilio→app: `setup` (incl. `customParameters`), `prompt` (`voicePrompt`, `lang`, `last`), `dtmf`, `interrupt` (`utteranceUntilInterrupt`, `durationUntilInterruptMs`), `error`. App→Twilio: `text` (stream tokens, `last: true` on the final one), `play`, `sendDigits`, `language` (switch TTS/STT language mid-call), `end` with `handoffData` — [Twilio ConversationRelay WebSocket messages](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/conversationrelay/websocket-messages)
- **Human handoff pattern (ConversationRelay):** the app sends `end` with `handoffData` (e.g. a JSON reason such as `"live-agent-handoff"`). Twilio posts it to the `<Connect>` action URL, and the handler returns new TwiML, such as dialing a live agent or enqueueing. Barge-in is set per message with `interruptible` (caller speech or DTMF stops playback) and `preemptible` (a later message stops current playback) — [Twilio ConversationRelay WebSocket messages](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/conversationrelay/websocket-messages)

**Latency added by the PSTN / SIP path (all vendor numbers, anecdotal)**
- Exotel (a telephony vendor) claims a traditional SIP path adds 80–150 ms before audio reaches ASR, with 3–5 intermediary hops, against 10–25 ms for its own WebSocket path. This is a self-interested comparison — [Exotel blog](https://exotel.com/blog/exotel-zero-latency-voice-ai-architecture/) **(anecdotal)**
- One vendor estimates one-way latency of about 30–80 ms for a domestic SIP trunk, 50–120 ms for domestic PSTN and 20–50 ms for same-region WebRTC. WebRTC jitter buffers add 40–100 ms of deliberate delay — [ConnexCS](https://connexcs.com/blog/webrtc-vs-sip-for-ai-voice-agents-which-architecture-scales); [Famulor](https://www.famulor.io/blog/webrtc-vs-sip-for-ai-voice-agents-2026-transport-guide) **(anecdotal)**
- Telephony audio is 8 kHz G.711. Feeding it to a 16 kHz STT model hurts accuracy, and transcoding costs latency. Teams often tune TTS but leave large endpointing timeouts (e.g. 700 ms) and playback buffers (e.g. 400 ms) untouched — [Smallest.ai blog](https://smallest.ai/blog/text-to-speech-for-phone-calls-latency-telephony-audio-and-turn-taking); [Coval](https://www.coval.dev/blog/voice-ai-latency) **(anecdotal)**
- Twilio's own latency guidance for webhooks: under 150 ms from EC2 test apps, under 100 ms for TwiML Bins. Cache static media, or use `<Say>` — [Twilio AMD docs](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/answering-machine-detection)

**Answering-machine / voicemail detection (AMD)**
- Twilio AMD modes. `Enable` returns `AnsweredBy` as soon as it decides (`machine_start`, `human`, `fax`, `unknown`). `DetectMessageEnd` returns `human` immediately but waits for the end of a machine greeting (`machine_end_beep|silence|other`), which is what you need to leave a voicemail. With `AsyncAmd=false` (the default) the call blocks until AMD finishes. With `true`, the call proceeds and the result arrives at `AsyncAmdStatusCallback` — [Twilio AMD docs](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/answering-machine-detection)
- Twilio AMD defaults: `MachineDetectionTimeout` 30 s (3–59), `SpeechThreshold` 2400 ms, `SpeechEndThreshold` 1200 ms, `SilenceTimeout` 5000 ms. Twilio says AMD "will not always return the right answer" (e.g. it can read a short voicemail greeting as a human) and publishes no accuracy figure. `MachineDetectionDuration` reports how long detection took — [Twilio AMD docs](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/answering-machine-detection)
- Telnyx sells standard AMD at $0.002/call and premium AMD at $0.0065/call. Twilio AMD is $0.0075/call — [Telnyx Voice API pricing](https://telnyx.com/pricing/call-control); [Twilio US voice pricing](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/voice/pricing/us)

**Caller-ID reputation: STIR/SHAKEN and spam labeling**
- FCC Eighth Caller ID Authentication Report and Order: a provider may hire a third party to do the technical signing, but must make all attestation decisions itself, sign with its own certificate, hold its own SPC token and keep written records. Compliance deadline **September 18, 2025**. Providers that miss it cannot claim STIR/SHAKEN implementation in the Robocall Mitigation Database — [TransNexus](https://transnexus.com/blog/2025/third-party-shaken-rules-date-reminder/); [FCC DA 25-730](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-730A1.pdf) **(secondary + primary PDF linked, not fetched)**
- On Aug 6, 2025 the FCC removed 185 providers from the Robocall Mitigation Database for noncompliance — [Mintz, 2025-08-28](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2025-08-28-telephone-and-texting-compliance-news-regulatory-update) **(secondary)**
- In 2025 the FCC **proposed** (FNPRM, scheduled for a vote on Oct 28, 2025) requiring verified caller name in the SHAKEN PASSporT for A-attested calls, passed through to handsets. Final status not found — [TransNexus](https://transnexus.com/blog/2025/fcc-proposes-shaken-caller-identity-rules/) **(secondary)**
- Spam labels come mainly from three analytics vendors, First Orion, Hiya and TNS. Carrier pairings reported: Hiya with AT&T, TNS with Verizon, First Orion with T-Mobile, though sources vary. Free Caller Registry registers a number with all three in one submission, but the registration must be renewed every 180 days. Hiya and First Orion run free business registration portals, and TNS has a dispute path — [SignalWire: resolving spam labels](https://signalwire.com/docs/platform/voice/resolving-spam-labels.md); [FirstDigital guide](https://learn.firstdigital.com/bin/view/FirstDigital%20Product%20Support/Calls%20Marked%20as%20Spam%3F/); [Consio help](https://help.consio.ai/en/articles/12549604-increase-answer-rates-register-your-us-number) **(secondary/vendor)**

- Numeracle told the FCC there is a conflict of interest, because Hiya and TNS also sell label-remediation services. It cited one contract at more than $40k/yr for 100 numbers. This is a competitor's allegation — [CommsRisk](https://commsrisk.com/?p=43299) **(anecdotal)**
- Twilio Branded Calling is $0.12/call — [Twilio US voice pricing](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/voice/pricing/us)

### Inferences
- **Telephony-only cost per minute** (outbound, media stream, recording, no AI), from list prices: Twilio about $0.014 + $0.0044 + $0.0025 ≈ **$0.021/min**. Telnyx about $0.007 + $0.0035 + $0.002 ≈ **$0.0125/min**. Managed STT+TTS relays (Twilio $0.07, Telnyx $0.05) cost several times the raw transport. The relay trade is managed turn-taking and barge-in in exchange for less control over the audio path, such as custom VAD or speech-to-speech models.
- A speech-to-speech model that expects 16/24 kHz PCM needs μ-law⇄PCM transcoding and resampling at the edge. If the CPaaS can emit L16 at 16 kHz (Plivo, Vonage), the decode step goes away, though the audio is still band-limited at the PSTN.
- Barge-in on raw Media Streams = detect caller speech → send `clear` → cancel LLM/TTS. Use `mark` to know how much audio actually played, so the transcript reflects what the caller heard.
- The CPaaS gives transfer primitives (Twilio `end`→TwiML `<Dial>`/queue, SIP REFER at $0.10). **Warm** transfer, where the agent briefs the human before bridging, has to be orchestrated: conference the human in, play or whisper the summary, then drop the AI leg. **Cold** transfer is a single REFER or `<Dial>`. No vendor doc fetched in this session documents warm transfer end to end (see Gaps).
- Blocking AMD with the 2400 ms default speech threshold can collide with the US 2-second abandonment rule for telemarketing (Q2). For outbound AI telemarketing, prefer async AMD plus an immediate compliant greeting.

- No AMD accuracy figures from any vendor.

## Q2. US compliance: TCPA and the FCC Feb 2024 AI-voice ruling, consent, revocation, DNC, abandonment, TSR, state recording and AI-disclosure laws

### Takeaway
In the US, an AI-generated voice on an **outbound** call legally counts as an "artificial or prerecorded voice" (FCC 24-17, effective Feb 8, 2024). The call therefore needs prior express consent, and prior express **written** consent if it is telemarketing. The caller must also follow the 47 CFR 64.1200(b) rules: identify the caller at the start, give a callback number, and offer an automated opt-out within 2 s for telemarketing. Revocation "by any reasonable means" must be honored within 10 business days (in force in the current eCFR). The 2023 "one-to-one" consent rule was vacated in January 2025 and is not in the current CFR text. The FCC's proposed AI-specific disclosure rules (Aug 2024 NPRM) are **not** shown as final. State law adds AI-disclosure and companion-chatbot statutes (CA AB 2905 effective Jan 1, 2025, for autodialed AI voice, plus a 2025–2027 wave of chatbot laws) and all-party recording-consent laws in about 11–13 states.

### Cited Findings

**FCC Declaratory Ruling FCC 24-17 (primary)**
- *Implications of AI Technologies on Protecting Consumers from Unwanted Robocalls and Robotexts*, CG Docket No. 23-362, **adopted Feb 2, 2024, released Feb 8, 2024, effective upon release** — [FCC 24-17 PDF](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-17A1.pdf)
- Holding (para. 5): "we confirm that the TCPA's restrictions on the use of 'artificial or prerecorded voice' encompass current AI technologies that resemble human voices and/or generate call content using a prerecorded voice. Therefore, callers must obtain prior express consent from the called party before making a call that utilizes artificial or prerecorded voice simulated or generated through AI technology." Voice clones are "'artificial' voice messages because a person is not speaking them" — [FCC 24-17 PDF](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-17A1.pdf)
- Footnote 13: if such robocalls "introduce an advertisement or contain telemarketing," the caller must obtain **prior express written consent** (47 CFR 64.1200(a)(2),(3)) — [FCC 24-17 PDF](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-17A1.pdf)
- Para. 8 extends the Soundboard ruling's reasoning: having a live agent pick which prerecorded or AI clip to play "does not negate" the prohibition. This covers AI that "wholly simulate[s] an artificial voice or resemble[s] the voice of a real person" — [FCC 24-17 PDF](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-17A1.pdf)
- Para. 9: the 64.1200(b) identification and disclosure rules and the opt-out mechanism "are applicable to any AI technology that initiates any outbound telephone call using an artificial or prerecorded voice to consumers." The ruling also lets State AGs pursue such callers under the TCPA — [FCC 24-17 PDF](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-17A1.pdf)
- Context: the ruling followed the January 2024 AI-cloned "Biden" robocalls to New Hampshire voters, and it adopted no new rules, only "clarified" the existing ones — [Mayer Brown, 2024-02-27](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2024/02/fcc-declares-authority-and-intent-to-regulate-ai-generated-calls-under-the-tcpa) **(secondary)**

**Current 47 CFR 64.1200 (primary, eCFR as of 2026-10-15)**
- **(b) Required content of every artificial or prerecorded voice message:** (1) "At the beginning of the message, state clearly the identity of the business… [its registered name]". (2) during or after the message, a callback number (not a 900 number), which for telemarketing must accept do-not-call requests during business hours. (3) for telemarketing and exempted calls to residential or wireless lines, "an automated, interactive voice- and/or key press-activated opt-out mechanism… within two (2) seconds of providing the identification information." The opt-out must automatically add the number to the do-not-call list and immediately end the call. Voicemail messages must include a toll-free number that reaches the same opt-out — [eCFR 47 CFR 64.1200](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-64/subpart-L/section-64.1200)
- **(a)(3) residential-line exemptions without consent:** emergency calls, non-commercial calls, commercial calls that are not telemarketing, tax-exempt nonprofit calls and certain HIPAA health-care messages. Most are capped at **three calls per consecutive 30 days** per line and require honoring opt-outs — [eCFR 47 CFR 64.1200](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-64/subpart-L/section-64.1200)
- **(a)(9) definition of prior express written consent:** a signed written agreement (e-signature valid under E-SIGN or state law) that "clearly authorizes the seller" to deliver telemarketing by ATDS or artificial/prerecorded voice, with the number, a clear and conspicuous disclosure, and a statement that consent is not a condition of purchase. The current text has **no** one-to-one or "logically and topically related" language — [eCFR 47 CFR 64.1200](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-64/subpart-L/section-64.1200)
- **(a)(10)–(12) revocation (in the current CFR):** consent may be revoked "by using any reasonable method." An automated voice or key-press opt-out on the call is "a reasonable means per se." Revocations "must be honored within a reasonable time not to exceed ten business days," and callers "may not designate an exclusive means." Other channels, such as voicemail or email, create a rebuttable presumption of revocation. A single confirmation text is permitted (presumed within consent if sent within 5 minutes) — [eCFR 47 CFR 64.1200](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-64/subpart-L/section-64.1200)
- **(c) DNC and calling hours:** no telephone solicitation to residential subscribers before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. local time at the called party's location, or to numbers on the National DNC Registry. Registrations are honored indefinitely. A safe harbor requires written procedures, training, an internal DNC list and registry access — [eCFR 47 CFR 64.1200](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-64/subpart-L/section-64.1200)
- **(a)(7) abandonment:** at most **3%** of live-answered telemarketing calls may be abandoned per 30-day campaign. A call is abandoned "if it is not connected to a live sales representative within two (2) seconds of the called person's completed greeting." If no rep is available within 2 s, a prerecorded identification and opt-out message must play — [eCFR 47 CFR 64.1200](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-64/subpart-L/section-64.1200)
- **One-to-one consent rule:** the Eleventh Circuit vacated it in *Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC* on **Jan 24, 2025**, before its Jan 27, 2025 effective date. The court held it exceeded the FCC's authority and also vacated the "logically and topically related" requirement — [Morrison Foerster, 2025-01-30](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/250130-eleventh-circuit-vacates-fcc-s-tcpa-one-to-one-consent-rule); [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/eleventh-circuit-vacates-tcpa-11-consent-rule) **(secondary)**. The current eCFR (a)(9) text above agrees.
- **FCC AI-specific NPRM (FCC 24-84, PROPOSED, not final):** adopted Aug 7, 2024. It proposes defining an "AI-generated call", requiring AI disclosure at the consent stage, and requiring a clear AI disclosure at the start of each AI-voice call, with exemptions for people with speech or hearing disabilities. Comments were due Oct 10 and replies Oct 25, 2024. The NPRM notes that "the TCPA's requirements do not extend to technologies used to answer inbound calls" — [Federal Register 2024-09-10](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-09-10/html/2024-19028.htm); [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/fcc-proposes-new-tcpa-rules-for-ai-calls-and-texts) **(secondary)**. No final Report and Order was found as of 2026-10-17.

**FTC Telemarketing Sales Rule (primary, eCFR as of 2026-10-15)**
- 16 CFR 310.4(b)(1)(v) bars outbound prerecorded-message calls that induce a purchase unless the seller holds an express written agreement. The agreement must follow a clear and conspicuous disclosure, must not be a condition of purchase, must name the specific seller, and must include the number and signature. The calls must also ring for at least 15 s or 4 rings, and within 2 s of the completed greeting must play the required disclosures and an automated opt-out — [eCFR 16 CFR 310.4](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-310/section-310.4)
- The FTC's TSR review "affirms existing prohibitions on robocalls using voice cloning technology" and extended telemarketing fraud protections to B2B calls (final rule, 2024) — [Public Citizen CLP blog](https://clpblog.citizen.org/new-ftc-rule-protects-businesses-from-telemarketing-fraud-and-affirms-protections-against-ai-enabled-scam-calls/) **(secondary)**
- FTC Government and Business Impersonation Rule took effect April 2024. In February 2024 the FTC **proposed** extending it to impersonating individuals, including liability for those who supply tools knowing they will be used to impersonate. Whether the extension was finalized is unconfirmed — [Mondaq: impersonation rule takes effect](https://mondaq.com/unitedstates/advertising-marketing-branding/1448066/ftcs-new-impersonation-rule-takes-effect-today); [WilmerHale, 2024-02-23](https://www.wilmerhale.com/insights/client-alerts/20240223-ftc-proposes-rule-to-make-impersonating-government-entities-and-businesses-unlawful) **(secondary)**

It is a high-risk algorithmic-discrimination law, not a call-disclosure law — [Orrick, 2026-04](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/04/2026-State-Chatbot-Laws-Key-Provisions-and-Regulatory-Trends) **(secondary)**
- One vendor tally: 19 states passed chatbot or companion bills out of 146 introduced across 2025–2026 — [Entagl / Verlingo state maps](https://www.entagl.com/blog/us-ai-chatbot-disclosure-laws-2026) **(vendor, unverified)**
- Federal **CHATBOT Act (H.R. 7985, 119th Congress)** is a pending bill. The earlier **R U REAL Act (H.R. 7120, 118th)**, which would have required TSR AI disclosures, is defunct — [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/04/2026-State-Chatbot-Laws-Key-Provisions-and-Regulatory-Trends); [congress.gov H.R. 7120](https://www.congress.gov/118/bills/hr7120/BILLS-118hr7120ih.htm)

**Call recording consent**
- Federal Wiretap Act (18 U.S.C. §2511) is one-party consent. The state lists commonly cited as **all-party** consent: CA, DE, FL, IL, MD, MA, MT, NV, NH, PA, WA (11), with some lists adding CT, MI and/or OR. The sources disagree and are not statutes — [Kilpatrick (KTS Law), 2024-07](https://ktslaw.com/en/Blog/GlobalPrivacy-and-CybersecurityLaw/2024/7/Wiretap-Laws-in-the-United-States) **(secondary)**; [Ringio help](https://help.ringio.com/en/articles/6314449-two-party-consent-states-call-recording) **(vendor)**

### Inferences
- **Outbound AI calls are robocalls.** The engineering defaults that follow: a consent ledger (who consented, when, exact disclosure text, seller name, number); written consent capture with e-signature for anything marketing-adjacent; an identity statement in the first utterance; a callback number; a working spoken and DTMF opt-out ("say stop or press 9") within 2 s of the identity statement on telemarketing calls; opt-out writes to the internal DNC list synchronously and ends the call; revocation from any channel propagates within 10 business days (build for minutes, not days); National DNC and internal-DNC scrubbing; and 8 a.m.–9 p.m. local-time gating on the callee's location.
- **Inbound AI agents** fall largely outside the TCPA, according to the FCC's own NPRM text. State AI-disclosure laws (Utah on request, Maine, the 2026–27 chatbot wave), all-party recording laws and the TSR still apply. Disclosing AI at call start costs little and meets every regime surveyed, so make it the universal default.
- Because AI voice counts as "artificial", "the AI greets, then transfers to a human" telemarketing still needs prior express written consent. The 2-second abandonment rule targets predictive dialers that do not connect a "live sales representative". Whether an AI agent can ever count as a live rep is unresolved and needs legal review. Under FCC 24-17 the conservative reading is that it cannot.
- Record every call in all-party states only after a disclosure such as "this call is recorded", and treat silence plus continuing as implied consent only where counsel approves. The simple build path is to announce recording on every call regardless of state.

- Whether the FTC finalized the individual-impersonation extension.

## Q3. EU/UK (and Hungary) compliance: GDPR basis for calls and recordings, ePrivacy unsolicited calls, EU AI Act Art. 50, UK PECR, Hungarian specifics

### Takeaway
**EU AI Act Art. 50(1)** has applied since **2 August 2026**. Providers of AI systems that interact directly with people must make sure people are told they are talking to an AI, "at the latest at the time of the first interaction", unless it is obvious. Breaches carry fines up to €15M or 3% of turnover. The Digital Omnibus (Reg. (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 July 2026) did **not** postpone Art. 50(1). It deferred only 50(2) machine-readable marking for systems already on the market, to 2 Dec 2026, plus the high-risk obligations. **ePrivacy Art. 13(1)** and **UK PECR reg. 19** require prior consent for automated calls for direct marketing. Live marketing calls are opt-out in the UK (TPS/CTPS). **Hungary** adds an opt-in rule for direct-marketing contact of natural persons (Grt. 6. §). For in-scope customer-service lines (Fgytv. 17/B. §) it also mandates recording of all calls with a unique ID, retention for 5 years, a notice at call start, the **live-human option first in the IVR menu** for complaints, and answer within 5 minutes.

### Cited Findings

**EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689)**
- Art. 50(1): providers shall ensure AI systems "intended to interact directly with natural persons" are designed so "the natural persons concerned are informed that they are interacting with an AI system," unless "obvious from the point of view of a natural person who is reasonably well-informed, observant and circumspect." Law-enforcement exception, except systems open to the public for reporting crimes — [artificialintelligenceact.eu Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/) (mirror of the official text. EUR-Lex fetch failed)
- Art. 50(5): information must be given "in a clear and distinguishable manner at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure" and meet accessibility requirements — [artificialintelligenceact.eu Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- Art. 50(3): deployers of emotion-recognition or biometric-categorisation systems must inform people exposed to them. This is relevant if a voice agent infers caller emotion — [artificialintelligenceact.eu Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- Application date: Art. 50 applies from **2 August 2026** under Art. 113 — [artificialintelligenceact.eu Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/); [Morgan Lewis, 2026-08](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2026/08/eu-ai-acts-transparency-rules-what-went-into-effect-on-2-august) **(secondary)**
- Digital Omnibus on AI: Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force **27 July 2026**. It moved Annex III high-risk obligations to 2 Dec 2027 and Annex I product-embedded ones to 2 Aug 2028. It gave generative systems already on the market by 2 Aug 2026 until **2 Dec 2026** for the Art. 50(2) machine-readable marking. It left the other Art. 50 duties unchanged — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/); [Morgan Lewis, 2026-08](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2026/08/eu-ai-acts-transparency-rules-what-went-into-effect-on-2-august); [Technology.org, 2026-10-13](https://www.technology.org/2026/10/13/eu-ai-act-deadlines-after-the-digital-omnibus-what-applies-from-december-2026-to-august-2028/) **(secondary. The Official Journal text was not fetched)**
- Commission Art. 50 guidelines adopted **20 July 2026**, and a voluntary code of practice on marking AI-generated content published **10 June 2026** — [nicfab](https://www.nicfab.eu/en/posts/ai-act-art50-guidelines/); [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/) **(secondary)**
- Penalties: Art. 99(4)(g) puts "transparency obligations for providers and deployers pursuant to Article 50" in the tier of up to **€15,000,000 or 3%** of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. Art. 99(6) gives SMEs the lower of the two — [artificialintelligenceact.eu Art. 99](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/99/)

**ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC (primary text via legislation.gov.uk mirror)**
- Art. 13(1): "The use of automated calling and communication systems without human intervention (automatic calling machines)… for the purposes of direct marketing may be allowed only in respect of subscribers or users who have given their prior consent." Art. 13(3): for other unsolicited direct-marketing communications, including live calls, Member States choose between opt-in and opt-out — [Directive 2002/58/EC Art. 13](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2002/58/article/13)

**UK PECR (primary + regulator)**
- PECR reg. 19: no recorded-matter direct marketing by an "automated calling or communication system" unless the subscriber has notified the caller of consent and the caller presents CLI or a contactable number. The definition covers systems that automatically initiate a sequence of calls and transmit "sounds which are not live speech" — [PECR reg. 19](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/19)
- ICO: automated-call consent "must specifically cover automated calls". General marketing or live-call consent is not enough. Every automated call must give the caller's name and a contact address or freephone number, and display the number. Live calls (regs 21, 21A, 21B) must not go to anyone who objected or to TPS/CTPS-registered numbers without specific consent. Claims-management calls need consent, and pensions cold calls are banned with narrow exceptions. "You must always say who is calling." The ICO says this guidance is **under review** following the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 — [ICO telephone marketing guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/telephone-marketing/)

**Hungary (primary statute text via njt.hu; NAIH guidance)**
- **Grt. (2008. évi XLVIII. tv.) 6. § (1)** says that unless another law provides otherwise, advertising by direct contact with a natural person ("közvetlen üzletszerzés"), "in particular" by email or an equivalent individual communication tool, may be sent "only if the recipient has given prior, unambiguous and express consent". Consent may be withdrawn at any time, free and without reasons, after which the data must be deleted at once (6. § (3)) — [njt.hu Grt.](https://njt.hu/jogszabaly/2008-48-00-00)
- **Fgytv. (1997. évi CLV. tv.) 17/B. § (3)** for the customer-service lines in scope: answer and begin substantive handling within **5 minutes** of the call connecting. The business "must put the choice of live-voice handling of complaints ['élőhangos ügyintézés'] in **first place** in the phone menu, without identifying the consumer and without advertising." "All oral complaints made by phone… and the telephone communication between the customer service and the consumer must be recorded." Recordings get a **unique ID** and are kept **5 years**. On request the consumer gets free playback and one free copy within 30 days, electronically if asked ((3a)–(3b)). The consumer must be informed of the recording, retention and ID "at the start of the telephone interaction" — [njt.hu Fgytv.](https://njt.hu/jogszabaly/1997-155-00-00)
- NAIH says the Fgytv. 17/B(3) recording duty binds public-service providers ("közszolgáltatási tevékenységet folytató vállalkozások") and others named by separate law. Telecoms carry separate duties: Eht. 141 § (fault reports kept ≥1 year) and NMHH Decree 2/2015 § 25 (complaints and fault reports recorded and kept 2 years, copy within 30 days). Banks must keep complaint-call recordings 5 years (Hpt. 288 § (4)) and insurers 1 year (Bit. 159 § (5)). Recording non-complaint calls needs another legal basis — [NAIH tájékoztató, 2016-05-09](https://www.naih.hu/files/2016_05_09_tajekoztato_hangfelvetelekrol.pdf). **Dated:** this guidance predates GDPR and reasons under the Infotv. consent model.
- NAIH treats voice and voice recordings as personal data, and the recording organisation as controller. Recordings may be kept only as long as the purpose requires and then deleted, backups included — [NAIH tájékoztató, 2016-05-09](https://www.naih.hu/files/2016_05_09_tajekoztato_hangfelvetelekrol.pdf)
- NAIH issued a 2024 opinion urging that a draft law fix the retention period instead of leaving it to the provider. Its outcome was not verified — [NAIH 10863-3/2024](https://naih.hu/files/naih-10863-3-2024-240806.pdf)

### Inferences
- **EU build default:** the first utterance on every call states that the caller is speaking with an AI system, plus who operates it. Art. 50(5) requires disclosure "at the latest" at first interaction, and a human-sounding voice on the phone is the opposite of "obvious". A company that builds and runs its own agent is likely the Art. 50(1) "provider". Confirm this with counsel, especially when a platform such as ElevenLabs, Vapi or Retell supplies the agent.
- If the agent infers emotion or sentiment from voice, Art. 50(3) disclosure applies to the deployer, and emotion recognition in some settings (e.g. workplace) falls under Art. 5 prohibitions. The second point is from general knowledge of the Act and was not fetched here, so get legal review.
- **Outbound AI marketing calls in the EU/UK** are "automated calling systems" with "sounds which are not live speech", which makes them opt-in. Synthetic speech is arguably not "live speech" under PECR reg. 19(4)(b). The UK route of live calls on opt-out plus TPS screening probably does **not** cover AI voice agents. This needs ICO or legal confirmation.
- **Hungary:** an AI agent on an in-scope Hungarian customer-service line must sit **behind**, or offer as the first IVR choice, a live-human complaint path. It must answer within 5 minutes, announce recording, retention and the unique ID at call start, record 100% of calls, and support 30-day free copy requests. For outbound marketing to natural persons, Grt. 6. § points to prior express consent. Whether "egyéni kommunikációs eszköz" covers phone calls should be confirmed by Hungarian counsel.
- **GDPR basis for recordings** (general knowledge, not fetched here, so verify): a statutory recording duty supports Art. 6(1)(c) legal obligation. Optional QA or model-training recording needs legitimate interest (with a balancing test and an objection right) or consent. Using call audio or transcripts to train models is a separate purpose that needs its own basis and a DPIA review. Voiceprints for identification are biometric special-category data (Art. 9).

- The Commission's 20 July 2026 Art. 50 guidelines were not read directly, including what they say about voice and phone agents and disclosure wording.
- No primary GDPR/EDPB text on lawful basis for call recording or AI call processing was fetched. NAIH's only recording guidance found is from 2016 (pre-GDPR).

- No NAIH or NMHH guidance specific to AI voice agents was found.
- UK Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 changes to PECR (fines, soft opt-in) were not detailed. The ICO says its guidance is under review.

## Q4. Industry practice: AI disclosure at call start, recording notices, opt-out handling, human escalation, prohibited uses

### Takeaway
The legal floors above converge on one call-opening pattern: say who is calling (the registered business name), that it is an AI, that the call is recorded and why, and how to stop or reach a human. That opening has to be followed by an opt-out that works immediately, by speech and DTMF. Impersonating real people with voice clones without consent is illegal on several fronts. That includes FCC 24-17 enforcement, FTC impersonation rules, and the TCPA when the call is a robocall made without consent.

### Cited Findings
- The FCC rules require identity "at the beginning of the message", a callback number, and an automated voice or key-press opt-out within 2 s of the identification for telemarketing. Opt-out must add the number to the DNC list and end the call immediately — [eCFR 47 CFR 64.1200(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-64/subpart-L/section-64.1200)
- An on-call automated or key-press opt-out is per se reasonable revocation. Callers may not restrict revocation to one channel — [eCFR 47 CFR 64.1200(a)(10)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-64/subpart-L/section-64.1200)
- The FCC's proposed rule (not final) would require AI disclosure both when consent is obtained and at the start of each AI-voice call — [Federal Register 2024-09-10](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-09-10/html/2024-19028.htm)
- EU: AI disclosure "at the latest at the time of the first interaction" — [AI Act Art. 50(5)](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- Hungary: recording notice at the start of the call, and the live-human complaint option first in the menu — [njt.hu Fgytv. 17/B](https://njt.hu/jogszabaly/1997-155-00-00)
- UK: "You must always say who is calling" and display a contactable number — [ICO telephone marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/telephone-marketing/)
- Impersonation: FCC 24-17 was prompted by an AI clone of President Biden. Voice cloning is treated as "artificial voice" — [FCC 24-17 PDF](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-17A1.pdf). The FTC Impersonation Rule (in effect April 2024) covers government and business impersonation, and an individual-impersonation extension was proposed — [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/insights/client-alerts/20240223-ftc-proposes-rule-to-make-impersonating-government-entities-and-businesses-unlawful) **(secondary)**. Tennessee SB 1580 bars AI from presenting as a licensed mental-health professional — [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/04/2026-State-Chatbot-Laws-Key-Provisions-and-Regulatory-Trends) **(secondary)**
- Human-escalation mechanics exist natively (ConversationRelay `end` + `handoffData` → TwiML route to a live agent) — [Twilio ConversationRelay WebSocket messages](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/conversationrelay/websocket-messages)
- Vendors push AI-disclosure scripts and state maps, but these are marketing content: [JustCall](https://justcall.io/blog/ai-voice-agent-disclosure-laws.html), [GetAssay](https://getassay.io/blog/state-by-state-ai-calling-laws) **(anecdotal)**

### Inferences
- **A call opening that meets every surveyed regime** (engineering suggestion, needs legal review per jurisdiction): "Hi, this is [AI agent name], an AI assistant calling on behalf of [registered business name]. This call is recorded for [purpose]. You can say 'stop' or press 9 at any time to opt out, or say 'agent' to reach a person." For outbound telemarketing this has to land within the 2-second windows, which means pre-synthesizing or caching the greeting audio rather than generating it after answer.
- **Hard engineering invariants:** opt-out keywords and DTMF are detected outside the LLM, with a deterministic intent check, so a model cannot ignore them. A DNC write completes before hang-up. Every utterance and consent event is logged and timestamped. A human-escalation path runs on every call and is the first IVR choice on in-scope Hungarian complaint lines. Agents never claim to be human when asked (Utah on-request disclosure, and Art. 50 in the EU). Voice clones of real people are used only with documented consent from that person.
- **Legal review required before launch** for: outbound campaigns of any kind (consent capture design), multi-state recording, any EU/UK outbound marketing, regulated sectors (health, finance, debt collection, insurance), and Hungarian customer-service lines.

### Gaps
- No primary industry standard was found, such as a CTIA, ATIS or ITU guideline for AI-agent call disclosures or a published carrier policy on AI outbound traffic.
- No data was found on whether AI disclosure at call start changes answer, hang-up or opt-out rates.
- Carrier and CPaaS acceptable-use rules for AI voice (e.g. Twilio AUP, Telnyx, Vonage policies on voice cloning or outbound AI) were not fetched.
